## Human studies and competing findings

### PMID:29510461 · favorable

Double-blind randomized placebo-controlled crossover. Population: People with ALS and symptomatic cramps. Intervention: Oral mexiletine 150 mg twice daily.

Primary endpoint: Muscle cramp frequency.

Observed outcome: Controlled evidence supports fewer symptomatic cramps.

Safety: Reported as generally well tolerated at this dose.

The clinical question was cramp relief. Alex's chart does not record cramps.
[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29510461/)

### PMID:26911633 · safety

Randomized double-blind dose-ranging study. Population: Sporadic ALS. Intervention: Oral mexiletine 300 or 900 mg/day.

Primary endpoint: Safety and tolerability.

Observed outcome: Dose-related decreases in cramps were observed as secondary effects. Progression estimates were imprecise.

Safety: The higher dose caused frequent discontinuation.

Relief of cramps and change in disease progression are different outcomes.
[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26911633/)

### PMID:25960085 · negative

Single-blind randomized add-on study. Population: 60 ALS participants. Intervention: Oral mexiletine with riluzole versus riluzole alone.

Primary endpoint: Six-month ALSFRS-R change.

Observed outcome: No functional benefit at 300 mg/day.

Safety: Reported adverse-event proportions were 33% and 20%.


[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25960085/)

### PMID:33340120 · biomarker

Randomized double-blind short physiology study. Population: 20 ALS participants. Intervention: Oral mexiletine or placebo.

Primary endpoint: Resting motor threshold.

Observed outcome: Electrophysiology findings do not establish slowed disability.

Safety: Four weeks of observation; long-term risks unresolved.


[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33340120/)
