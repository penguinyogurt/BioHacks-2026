## Why would changing delivery change the result?

**Original delivery**. Increasing regional exposure has no demonstrated basis for reversing the faster functional decline observed at the tested systemic regimen. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17980667/)

**Proposed focused ultrasound**. No reviewed drug-specific study showed that motor-cortex sonication corrects the relevant exposure or engagement limitation for this clinical outcome. The four-person ALS feasibility study measured temporary gadolinium enhancement. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31558719/)

## Human studies and competing findings

### PMID:17980667 · harm

Double-blind placebo-controlled randomized phase III. Population: 412 people with ALS. Intervention: Oral minocycline titrated to 400 mg/day for nine months.

Primary endpoint: Rate of ALSFRS-R decline.

Observed outcome: Function declined faster on minocycline: 1.30 versus 1.04 ALSFRS-R points/month, p=0.005.

Safety: Gastrointestinal and neurologic adverse events were also more common.

Mortality and FVC differences were not statistically significant. Preclinical neuroprotection did not predict the human result.
[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17980667/)

### PMID:16193258 · safety

Small randomized add-on pilot. Population: Twenty ALS patients. Intervention: Oral minocycline 100 mg/day plus riluzole versus riluzole alone.

Primary endpoint: Combination safety; serial function and respiratory assessment.

Observed outcome: No significant adverse effects were detected in this pilot.

Safety: Twenty patients and six months of follow-up give limited power for safety.

The subsequent larger phase III minocycline trial documented functional harm.
[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16193258/)

### PMID:15159491 · safety

Two small blinded placebo-controlled feasibility studies. Population: 19 and 23 ALS participants. Intervention: Oral minocycline 200 mg/day or escalating to 400 mg/day.

Primary endpoint: Tolerability and feasible dose.

Observed outcome: Pilot feasibility did not establish benefit.

Safety: Laboratory elevations and a gastrointestinal-event trend occurred at the higher regimen.


[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15159491/)

### PMID:18608093 · combination

Randomized active-combination selection design. Population: ALS participants in a phase II selection trial. Intervention: Oral celecoxib/creatine versus minocycline/creatine.

Primary endpoint: Six-month ALSFRS-R decline used to select a combination.

Observed outcome: Celecoxib/creatine was selected over minocycline/creatine; effects of individual ingredients could not be isolated.

Safety: Rash was more frequent with celecoxib/creatine.

A historical comparison did not establish placebo-controlled benefit.
[pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18608093/)
